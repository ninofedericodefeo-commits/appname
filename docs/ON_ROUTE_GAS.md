# Gas on your drive

The Gas screen links to a route search. Enter a destination address, latitude/longitude, or a supported full Apple Maps or Google Maps directions link. The app gets the current location, asks the device's geocoder for address coordinates if needed, then requests a driving route from the public OSRM demo server. For trips of 60 miles or more, it searches mapped fuel stations near the next 120 miles of the route. Shorter trips offer an explicit **Find gas on this trip anyway** button.

Mapped station locations come from public Overpass instances and OpenStreetMap. The app also includes locations from reports saved on this device. A station is shown only when it is within about one straight-line mile of the route. This is not a road detour calculation; highways, exits, closures, and access can make a stop impractical. Only locally saved receipt or direct price reports can be ranked. When none exist, the screen shows on-route station locations and says prices are unknown. The fictional $9.99 placeholder is never a recommendation. If public map lookup fails, saved locations along the route can still appear with a warning.

The app cannot inspect an active route inside Apple Maps or Google Maps. It accepts a destination the user enters or copies from a supported link. Google Maps can open a route through a selected station; Apple Maps opens directions to the station. These free public routing and map services may be unavailable or rate limited, so the app shows a retryable error instead of inventing stations or prices. Before driving, check the route and price in Maps and at the pump.
