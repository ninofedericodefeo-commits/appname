# Gas on your drive

The Gas screen links to a route search. Enter a destination address, latitude/longitude, or a supported full Apple Maps or Google Maps directions link. Also enter estimated miles left in the tank, or gallons left plus estimated MPG. The app gets the current location, asks the device's geocoder for address coordinates if needed, then requests a driving route from the public OSRM demo server. It aims for a stop before the estimated empty point, leaving a fuel buffer, and searches a segment near that point. On a trip of at least 60 miles, this search starts automatically when fuel is needed. Shorter trips and trips where the estimated range reaches the destination offer **Find gas on this trip anyway**.

Mapped station locations come from public Overpass instances and OpenStreetMap. The app also includes locations from reports saved on this device. A station is shown only when it is within about one straight-line mile of the route and before the estimated safe range. This is not a road detour calculation; highways, exits, closures, and access can make a stop impractical. Stops within about 25 miles before the target are preferred. Local receipt and direct price reports help rank them; when no prices are saved, the app orders locations near the target and says prices are unknown. The fictional $9.99 placeholder is never a recommendation. If public map lookup fails, saved locations along the route can still appear with a warning.

The app cannot inspect an active route inside Apple Maps or Google Maps. It accepts a destination the user enters or copies from a supported link. Google Maps can open a route through a selected station; Apple Maps opens directions to the station. These free public routing and map services may be unavailable or rate limited, so the app shows a retryable error instead of inventing stations or prices. Before driving, check the route and price in Maps and at the pump.
